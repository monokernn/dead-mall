# Meridian, 2004

## Experience

A first-person walk through a fictional American shopping mall in October 2004. The public spaces are empty, the escalators have stopped, the fountain is dry, but some lights are still on. The clock reads 8:17 PM.

The mood is familiarity, solitude and mild unease. An attentive first visit may take 10–15 minutes; there is no timer. The player is free to choose a route and revisit places.

All visitor-facing content is in English: the interface, accessibility labels, navigation, storefronts, posters, menus, price labels and errors. Period prices use US dollars and cents.

## Layout

Two levels around a central atrium. A lower ring connects west and east galleries. Upstairs, a continuous balcony leads to a food court. Two stopped escalators connect the levels in both directions.

The entrance is to the south, Orbit Amusements is to the west, the photo booth is to the east and the food court is to the north on level 2.

Required landmarks: dry fountain, shuttered stores, two escalators, five arcade cabinets with one illuminated CRT, three food counters, one remaining chair and a photo booth.

Three primary compositions: the fountain and escalators from the entrance; the lone chair facing closed food counters; a gallery of shutters leading toward arcade light.

## Art direction

Late-1990s mall architecture, still recognizable in 2004: cream ceramic tile, muted teal, dusty mauve, brass rails, frosted glazing and suspended ceiling panels. Wear follows use: scuffed floors, dull metal, faded print and dirt around edges.

Light comes from a cool skylight, scattered fluorescent tubes, warm menu boards and one arcade screen. Darker areas remain navigable. Empty floor area is a deliberate part of the composition.

Fictional identities include Sunvale Records, Dayframe Photo, Cedar & Thread, Soft Season, Halfstep, Homeward, Paper Parade, Sundial Optics, Orbit Amusements, Golden Fold, NOODLE No. 2 and MOON SODA.

## Story and interaction

Explore, observe and listen. Stage 4 includes six readable items, one arcade-button response and a staff-door response. Clues include the October 16 event poster, fountain maintenance, a store relocation, token pricing, a promised November food-court renovation and an instruction to switch off advertising lights.

No enemies, jump scares, compulsory objectives, inventory, multiplayer or fully accessible store interiors. The reason for closure is left open.

Audio for stage 4: ventilation, lamp hum, surface-dependent footsteps, atrium reverberation, distant electronics and restrained ambient music. Implemented procedurally in Web Audio during stage 4; no recorded music or audio downloads are required.
